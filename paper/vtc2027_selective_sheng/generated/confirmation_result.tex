We next test the frozen rule on independent paired observations (Table~\ref{tab:main} and Fig.~\ref{fig:quality}). The selector changes mean PSNR by +1.334 dB relative to calibrated joint sampling, +0.750 dB relative to blind Gaussian denoising, and +1.140 dB relative to the same-gate control. These observed averages favor selection over both fixed receivers and the tested bypass control on the confirmation set. Estimating total disturbance changes the Gaussian receiver from 18.829 to 20.843 dB, so the thermal-noise-only port is insufficient as the sole Gaussian baseline.

To examine where selection helps, we compare fixed receivers across profiles. Under stationary interference, Gaussian denoising reaches 21.310 dB, compared with 19.282 dB for joint sampling. Under burst interference, joint sampling reaches 21.317 dB, compared with 20.481 dB for Gaussian denoising. The selector reaches 21.317, 21.285, and 22.177 dB for stationary, alternating, and burst interference, respectively. The reversed ordering of the fixed receivers supports adapting the disturbance model. It does not establish a causal interpretation of either energy statistic.

For external context, matched direct-codec anchors reach 17.394 dB for DeepJSCC and 16.923 dB for MambaJSCC. They use independently trained latent spaces and serve only as external codec references. One training seed and a fixed 40-epoch budget do not establish convergence or a general architecture ranking. Without fine-tuning, public DeepJSCC (ImageNet/SNR 19), MambaJSCC (CLIC2021/AWGN 10), and MambaJSCC (DIV2K/Rayleigh) weights yield 13.755, 17.020, and 16.650 dB, respectively. Their protocol mismatches preclude a controlled claim about retraining benefit.
