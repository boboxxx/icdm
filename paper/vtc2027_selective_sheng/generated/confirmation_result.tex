We next test the frozen rule on independent paired observations (Table~\ref{tab:main} and Fig.~\ref{fig:quality}). The selector changes mean PSNR by +1.334 dB relative to calibrated joint sampling, +0.750 dB relative to blind Gaussian denoising, and +1.140 dB relative to the same-gate control. The gain over the same-gate control distinguishes changing the recovery model from the tested bypass alone. Estimating total disturbance raises the Gaussian receiver from 18.829 to 20.843 dB, showing the value of accounting for interference power.

To examine where selection helps, we compare fixed receivers across profiles. Under stationary interference, Gaussian denoising reaches 21.310 dB, compared with 19.282 dB for joint sampling. Under burst interference, joint sampling reaches 21.317 dB, compared with 20.481 dB for Gaussian denoising. The selector reaches 21.317, 21.285, and 22.177 dB for stationary, alternating, and burst interference, respectively. This reversal creates an opportunity for observation-dependent receiver choice.

Matched direct-codec references reach 17.394 dB for DeepJSCC and 16.923 dB for MambaJSCC in their independently trained latent spaces. These results describe the single-seed, 40-epoch training budget; convergence and architecture-wide rankings remain untested. Without fine-tuning, public DeepJSCC (ImageNet/SNR 19), MambaJSCC (CLIC2021/AWGN 10), and MambaJSCC (DIV2K/Rayleigh) weights yield 13.755, 17.020, and 16.650 dB, respectively. The training and protocol differences make these values transfer diagnostics.
