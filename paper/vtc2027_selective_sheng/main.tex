\begin{abstract}
We present a blind disturbance-model selector for diffusion-aided semantic
image reception. Given a received latent, the selector uses excess energy and
block-energy heterogeneity to choose Gaussian denoising or joint
signal--interference reconstruction before diffusion inference. This choice
addresses a practical limitation of joint reconstruction: estimating an
unknown interferer can add both reconstruction error and computation. We fit
the rule on separate development images and then freeze it together with all
neural weights. Only the selected receiver is executed. We evaluate the rule
on CelebA images with CIFAR-10 interference, using identical observations for
all receiver comparisons and a same-gate joint receiver as a control.
\input{generated/abstract_result.tex}
Separately trained DeepJSCC and MambaJSCC codecs provide external performance
references under the same channel-use budget.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
We consider semantic image reception under co-channel interference of unknown
power and structure. Deep joint source--channel coding (JSCC) maps images
directly to channel symbols and degrades gradually as channel noise increases
\cite{bourtsoulatze2019deep}. SwinJSCC and MambaJSCC implement this principle
with transformer and state-space architectures
\cite{yang2025swinjscc,wu2024mambajscc}. Even so, a deployed codec must recover
images from unmodeled interference without changing the transmitted latent or
adding unnecessary receiver computation.

Generative receivers use learned priors to recover corrupted latents. CDDM
matches the aggregate disturbance to a diffusion noise level and denoises the
received latent \cite{wu2024cddm}. ICDM instead reconstructs the desired and
interfering latents jointly \cite{wu2026icdm}. Iterative source error correction
\cite{pmlr-v206-lee23c}, channel-conditioned diffusion reconstruction
\cite{wang2025diffcom}, and blind inverse-problem inference
\cite{murata2023gibbsddrm} provide related forms of receiver adaptation. These
methods provide recovery mechanisms, but they do not resolve which disturbance
model a receiver should use for a particular observation.

Joint reconstruction is preferable only when the recovered interference
structure offsets its estimation error. The receiver must otherwise infer two
latents and an unknown amplitude from one mixture using imperfect priors and
finite-step sampling. An inaccurate interference estimate can distort the
desired latent and still consume twice the predictor evaluations. Gaussian
denoising avoids this burden, but discards structure that can aid recovery. We
therefore ask whether the receiver can exploit joint reconstruction only when
the received frame supports its additional model assumptions.

Our key observation is that a richer disturbance model contributes both
structure and estimation uncertainty. The preferred model can therefore vary
from frame to frame. Excess received energy measures aggregate disturbance
relative to the known signal and noise powers. Block-energy variation measures
how unevenly that energy is distributed. Both statistics are available before
diffusion inference and do not require identifying the interfering image.
Because desired-signal variation also affects them, their value must be
established on held-out images.

We address this decision with a blind selector based on the two statistics.
Image-group validation compares energy-only and two-feature threshold rules on
separate development images. It retains a fixed receiver when selection gives
no validation gain. The resulting rule is frozen and executes one branch per
frame. All neural weights remain fixed, isolating receiver selection from codec
or prior retraining. The experiment therefore asks a narrow question: whether
observation-dependent receiver choice improves a fixed generative receiver
stack.

On 256 independent image pairs, the selector improves mean PSNR by 0.750 dB
over blind Gaussian denoising and 1.334 dB over calibrated joint sampling. It
also uses 34.7\% fewer predictor evaluations than joint sampling. A same-gate
control distinguishes model selection from merely bypassing joint inference,
while harmful decisions and block mismatch expose its limits. Separately
trained DeepJSCC and MambaJSCC codecs provide external reference points. The
shared-prior receiver ports do not reproduce the complete published CDDM or
ICDM systems.

To summarize, we make three contributions. (1) We formulate disturbance-model
selection as a blind reconstruction decision for a frozen semantic codec.
(2) We develop an $O(n)$ energy-based rule that selects one receiver before
generative inference without neural retraining. (3) We evaluate the frozen
rule on paired independent observations, establishing its mean-quality and
computation benefit while measuring harmful decisions and mismatch sensitivity.
Sections II and III present the system model and selector; Sections IV and V
report experiments and conclusions.

\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}
In this section, we first model image transmission, then introduce the two
diffusion-assisted recovery models, and finally formulate receiver selection.

\subsection{Image Representation and Transmission}
Let $\bm u\in[0,1]^d$ be an RGB image, with $d=3H_{\rm img}W_{\rm img}$.
The JSCC encoder $f_s$ combines representation learning and channel mapping;
$f_z$ encodes an interfering image $\bm v$ into the same dimension:
\begin{equation}
 \bm x=\mathcal N(f_s(\bm u)),\qquad
 \bm z=\mathcal N(f_z(\bm v)),\quad
 \mathcal N(\bm t)=\frac{\sqrt n\,\bm t}{\norm{\bm t}}.
 \label{eq:encode}
\end{equation}
Each nonzero codeword contains $n$ complex symbols with unit average power,
giving bandwidth ratio $n/d$. We partition the symbols into $B$ blocks of
lengths $L_b$, where $\sum_bL_b=n$. The received symbols are
\begin{equation}
 \bm y_b=\bm x_b+a_b\bm z_b+\bm w_b,\qquad
 \bm w\sim\mathcal{CN}(\bm0,N_0\bm I),\quad a_b\ge0 .
 \label{eq:channel}
\end{equation}
Here $a_b$ denotes the unknown interference amplitude in block $b$, and $N_0$
denotes the complex noise power. We assume unit desired-channel gain and known
symbol alignment. The receiver knows $N_0$, the normalization, and the block
boundaries, but not $\bm u$, $\bm z$, or $a_b$. For nominal SINR $\gamma$, the
simulator sets the average interference power to $10^{-\gamma/10}-N_0$.
Realized block energies of $\bm z$ make the per-frame SINR differ from this
nominal setting.

\subsection{Diffusion-Assisted Image Recovery}
The receiver combines the frame-specific evidence in $\bm y$ with learned
latent priors. Joint reconstruction uses both priors:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 p(\bm x,\bm z\mid\bm y,\bm a)&\propto p_s(\bm x)p_z(\bm z)\\
 &\quad\times\exp\!\left[-\frac{1}{N_0}\sum_b
 \norm{\bm y_b-\bm x_b-a_b\bm z_b}^{2}\right].
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}
The amplitudes must be estimated. Moreover, a matching mixture cannot identify
both latents: $(\bm x_b+a_b\bm\delta_b,\bm z_b-\bm\delta_b)$ produces the same
observation. Gaussian recovery instead uses only $p_s$:
\begin{equation}
 p_{\G}(\bm y\mid\bm x;\nu)=
 \mathcal{CN}(\bm y;\bm x,\nu\bm I),\qquad \nu\ge N_0 .
 \label{eq:gaussian}
\end{equation}
The Gaussian and joint receivers, $\mathcal R_{\G}$ and $\mathcal R_{\SIC}$,
use frozen diffusion priors with observation-based initialization or guidance.
Neither performs exact posterior sampling, and only joint recovery estimates a
second latent. A blind rule $\pi_\theta(\bm y)\in\{\G,\SIC\}$ selects one branch.
The common decoder then reconstructs
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\bm u}_\theta
 =g_s\!\left(\mathcal N\!\left(\mathcal R_{\pi_\theta(\bm y)}(\bm y)\right)\right).
 \label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Quality, Cost, and Receiver Selection}
We measure image reconstruction with per-image PSNR, $q(\bm u,\widehat{\bm u})=
-10\log_{10}(\|\bm u-\widehat{\bm u}\|^2/d)$ for clipped RGB outputs.
LPIPS and MS-SSIM provide additional fidelity measures; they do not measure
downstream task accuracy. We measure computation by counting calls to both
priors, and receiver latency includes selection and decoding.

For development pairs $\mathcal D=\{(\bm u_i,\bm y_i)\}_{i=1}^{M}$, we fit
\begin{equation}
 \theta^\star\in\arg\max_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}q(\bm u_i,\widehat{\bm u}_\theta(\bm y_i)),
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $\Theta$ contains the rule family retained by image-group validation and
a constant-receiver fallback. Bandwidth, power, and neural weights remain
fixed; we report computation separately from the PSNR objective. This fitting
is offline. At inference, the frozen rule executes one branch without access
to its reconstruction error. Section III develops the observable decision.

\section{Blind Disturbance-Model Selection}
In this section, we first describe the two candidate receivers and then develop
a blind rule that selects one before diffusion inference.

\subsection{Candidate Receivers}
Both receivers share the frozen desired-signal prior and decoder. The Gaussian
receiver first estimates aggregate disturbance from the received symbols:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 e_b=\frac{\norm{\bm y_b}^2}{L_b},\qquad
 \bar e=\frac{1}{n}\sum_b L_be_b,\\
 \widehat P=\pos{\bar e-1-N_0},\qquad
 \widehat\nu=N_0+\widehat P.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
With unit source power and a vanishing signal--interference cross expectation,
unclipped excess energy estimates the mean interference energy. Finite blocks,
latent correlation, and clipping make this estimate inexact. In particular,
local desired-signal energy can resemble interference heterogeneity.

Let $r(\bm y)\in\R^{2n}$ stack real and imaginary components. Given the training
diffusion schedule $\bar\alpha_t$, the Gaussian receiver $\G$ starts at the
nearest noise level satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1-\bar\alpha_{t_0}}{\bar\alpha_{t_0}}
 \simeq\frac{\widehat\nu}{2},\qquad
 \bm v_{t_0}=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t_0}}\,r(\bm y).
 \label{eq:match}
\end{equation}
The factor $1/2$ converts complex noise power to variance per real coordinate.
For decreasing respaced indices, a noise predictor $\epsilon_s$ gives
\begin{align}
 \widehat{\bm x}_0&=
 \frac{\bm v_t-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon_s(\bm v_t,t)}
 {\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}},\\
 \bm v_{t'}&=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t'}}\widehat{\bm x}_0+
 \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{t'}}\epsilon_s(\bm v_t,t).
 \label{eq:gaussian_update}
\end{align}
The receiver uses at most 40 predictor evaluations and fewer when the matched
initial index is below 39.

The joint receiver $\SIC$ initializes
$a_b^{(0)}=\sqrt{\pos{e_b-1-N_0}}$ and performs joint diffusion inference with
signal and interference predictors. During late corrector updates it obtains
denoised, frame-normalized estimates $\widetilde{\bm x}$ and
$\widetilde{\bm z}$, then updates
\begin{equation}
 a_b^{+}=\pos{\frac{
 \Re\{\widetilde{\bm z}_b^{H}
       (\bm y_b-\widetilde{\bm x}_b)\}}
 {\norm{\widetilde{\bm z}_b}^{2}}}.
 \label{eq:amplitude}
\end{equation}
An empty denominator retains the previous amplitude. This nonnegative
least-squares update is conditional on point estimates and does not correct
their uncertainty. Calibration begins when the signal coefficient
$\alpha(t)\ge0.5$. Estimated block SINR updates the inherited guidance
coefficients by clipped linear interpolation of the original AWGN table.
The existing 40-step, order-three sampler calls each prior 40 times. An
initialization-only variant keeps the amplitudes fixed and isolates the effect
of calibration.

\subsection{Selection Before Generative Inference}
The selector combines $\widehat P$ with normalized block-energy variation:
\begin{equation}
 H=\frac{B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^{B}(e_b-\bar e)^2}
          {\max(\bar e^2,\varepsilon)}.
 \label{eq:heterogeneity}
\end{equation}
Here $\varepsilon>0$ prevents division by zero, and all receiver blocks have
equal length. The statistic $H$ measures energy variation, not the interference
distribution. For $\theta=(\tau_\ell,\tau_h,\eta)$, the rule is
\begin{equation}
 \pi_\theta(\bm y)=
 \begin{cases}
 \G,&\widehat P\le \tau_\ell,\ H\le\eta,\\
 \G,&\widehat P\le \tau_h,\ H>\eta,\\
 \SIC,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:selector}
\end{equation}
We also test the reversed assignment of $\G$ and $\SIC$ in
\eqref{eq:selector}; thus, the Gaussian branch is not assigned to the low-power
region a priori. The energy-only family sets $\tau_\ell=\tau_h$. Either family
can reduce to a constant receiver, allowing validation to reject adaptation.

We fit the thresholds to mean per-image PSNR on development data. Eight folds
keep all channel conditions for an image together. For each fold, thresholds
are fitted on the other seven folds and evaluated on the held-out images. We
then refit the family with the highest out-of-fold mean on all development
images. Ties favor lower inference cost and then the simpler family. If neither
family improves on the better fixed receiver, validation retains that receiver.
Confirmation and pressure results never alter the thresholds. At inference,
the selector evaluates \eqref{eq:selector} and executes only the chosen branch.
This adds $O(n)$ energy calculations and no diffusion evaluations.

For analysis, let $q_j(\bm u,\bm y)$ be a candidate's PSNR. The unavailable
per-frame oracle has quality $\max(q_\G,q_\SIC)$. The exact identity
\begin{equation}
 \max(q_\G,q_\SIC)-q_{\pi_\theta}
 =|q_\G-q_\SIC|\,
 \mathbf1\{\pi_\theta\ne\arg\max_j q_j\}
 \label{eq:regret}
\end{equation}
shows that complementary receivers alone are insufficient: errors on large-gap
frames dominate regret. This identity is an accounting relation, not a
generalization bound. The fitting objective is mean PSNR; it does not optimize
mean MSE, LPIPS, or harmful-tail probability.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
In this section, we compare reconstruction quality and inference cost with
fixed receivers and external codecs. We then examine harmful decisions and
interference mismatch.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
We use $128\times128$ CelebA center crops as desired images
\cite{liu2015celeba};
$32\times32$ CIFAR-10 interference images are nearest-neighbor upsampled.
Training uses 162,770 desired and 50,000 interference images. The frozen
SwinJSCC codec has stage widths $(128,192,256)$, depths $(2,2,6)$, and an
$8\times16\times16$ real latent: 1,024 complex symbols at CBR $1/48$.
Each DiT prior has 28 blocks, width 1,152, 16 heads and patch size two.
The inherited training schedule uses 40/100 desired/interference-codec epochs
and 50/20 desired/interference-prior epochs; selection adds no neural training.

For external references, DeepJSCC and MambaJSCC each train desired and
interference codecs for 40 epochs at SNR 20 dB and CBR $1/48$, using one seed
and validation MSE for checkpoint selection. DeepJSCC uses a pinned third-party
PyTorch reproduction; MambaJSCC uses official code. Public checkpoints are
also evaluated without fine-tuning, with one encoder processing both image
streams. Public MambaJSCC differs in training domain, channel and resolution;
public DeepJSCC additionally uses CBR $0.16256$. We treat these results as
transfer diagnostics. The SwinJSCC-specific diffusion priors cannot be attached
to either codec without new prior training. We do not reproduce the complete
published CDDM or ICDM systems.

Development uses 64 validation pairs and seed 1700. For independent
confirmation, we use 256 test pairs, seeds 2700/2701, SNR 20 dB, and nominal SINRs
$\{-7,-4,0,4,7,10\}$ dB. Sixteen 64-symbol blocks have stationary,
alternating $(0.25,1.75)$, or burst $(0,2)$ relative interference powers.
These 18 conditions give 9,216 frames per receiver. We run confirmation only
after freezing the policy. File hashes exclude overlap with development,
pressure tests, and
200 previously evaluated pairs; independence is at image-pair, not identity,
level. The power grid is $\{0,0.1,0.2,0.35,0.5,0.75,1,1.5,2,3,4,8,10^6\}$;
the heterogeneity grid is $\{0.05,0.1,0.2,0.4,0.8,1.6\}$, giving 26 energy-only
and 2,028 two-feature rules across both assignment directions.

We compare with direct decoding, Gaussian reception using $N_0$ alone,
blind Gaussian reception, initialization-only and calibrated joint sampling,
and the selector. The same-gate control uses direct decoding for
$\widehat P\le0.15$ and calibrated joint sampling otherwise. Its outputs and
component times are reconstructed from paired branches, excluding gate
overhead. Development also tests coupled feedback and a Gaussian receiver
given nominal disturbance power, a privileged diagnostic. All SwinJSCC variants
share received tensors and sampler seeds; external codecs share image pairs,
conditions and channel seeds but generate their own codewords.

We measure all receivers in float32 with batch size one, TF32 disabled, four
CPU threads, and an RTX 4090. Synchronized receiver time includes selection,
sampling, and decoding;
encoding, metric computation and loading are excluded. The number of function
evaluations (NFE) counts calls to both priors.
Quality metrics are per-image PSNR and MSE, LPIPS-VGG v0.1 \cite{zhang2018lpips},
and four-scale MS-SSIM \cite{wang2003msssim}, averaged arithmetically after RGB
clipping to $[0,1]$; four scales fit the image resolution. Paired 95\% intervals
use 5,000 bootstrap resamples of image pairs, retaining both seeds and all
conditions. A harmful decision loses more than 0.5 dB against its comparator.
