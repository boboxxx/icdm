\begin{abstract}
Explicit interference reconstruction is useful only when its additional
modeling assumptions improve the desired image estimate. With unknown
interference power, a joint diffusion sampler may instead introduce errors
and unnecessary computation. We propose a blind disturbance-model selector
for a frozen semantic image codec. Before diffusion inference, the receiver
uses excess received energy and block-energy heterogeneity to choose either
Gaussian denoising or joint signal--interference reconstruction. The rule is
fitted on separate development images; only the selected branch is executed,
and no neural weights are updated. We evaluate the rule on CelebA images with
CIFAR-10 interference, using identical received signals for all receiver
comparisons and a joint receiver with the same bypass as an additional control.
\input{generated/abstract_result.tex}
Separately trained DeepJSCC and MambaJSCC codecs provide external performance
references under the same channel-use budget.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Deep joint source--channel coding (JSCC) transmits an image by mapping its
pixels directly to channel symbols. The encoder and decoder are trained
together, enabling reconstruction quality to degrade gradually as the channel
worsens \cite{bourtsoulatze2019deep}. SwinJSCC uses transformer features for
image transmission \cite{yang2025swinjscc}, while MambaJSCC adopts a state-space
architecture \cite{wu2024mambajscc}. These learned codecs address the bandwidth
and noise constraints of wireless image transmission. A deployed receiver,
however, may also encounter co-channel interference whose strength and
structure differ from the channel used in training.

Generative receivers can improve reconstruction without redesigning the
transmitted representation. CDDM matches the channel disturbance to a diffusion
noise level and denoises the received latent \cite{wu2024cddm}. ICDM explicitly
models the desired and interfering latents and recovers them jointly
\cite{wu2026icdm}. Receiver adaptation has also been studied through iterative
source error correction \cite{pmlr-v206-lee23c}, channel-conditioned diffusion
reconstruction \cite{wang2025diffcom}, and blind estimation of forward-model
parameters \cite{murata2023gibbsddrm}. These methods motivate using learned
priors at reception, but leave a practical choice for a receiver equipped with
both denoising and interference-reconstruction capabilities.

Explicit separation need not be preferable on every received frame. A Gaussian
approximation ignores the interferer's structure, yet avoids estimating a
second latent signal and its unknown amplitude. Joint reconstruction exploits
that structure but must infer two signals from one mixture using imperfect
priors and finite-step sampling. Errors in the inferred interferer can distort
the desired signal even when the reconstructed mixture fits the observation.
Always invoking the joint receiver can therefore increase both reconstruction
error and computation. Conversely, always denoising the aggregate disturbance
can discard structure that would help recover the image.

This paper studies when to use each receiver while keeping the codec and
learned priors fixed. The choice must be made from the received symbols,
without the clean image or true interference power. Evaluating both branches
would incur both inference costs, and their true reconstruction errors are
unavailable at reception. We instead use excess received energy and its
variation across symbol blocks to select one branch before diffusion sampling.
A threshold rule is fitted on separate development images, after which its
parameters remain fixed. The selected latent estimate is passed to the common
image decoder.

The contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item We formulate blind disturbance-model selection between Gaussian denoising
and joint signal--interference reconstruction for a frozen codec, requiring
only $O(n)$ energy calculations before the chosen receiver runs.
\item We fit energy-only and two-feature rules by image-group validation, with
a fixed-receiver fallback when validation finds no selection gain.
\item We evaluate the frozen rule on independent image pairs using identical
received signals, a blind total-disturbance Gaussian baseline, and a joint
receiver with the same direct-decoding gate; the comparison includes quality,
inference cost, and harmful decisions.
\end{itemize}
The controls distinguish model selection from improved power estimation or
simply bypassing separation. Separately trained DeepJSCC and MambaJSCC codecs
provide external references, while public checkpoints test transfer without
fine-tuning. The Gaussian and joint receivers share frozen priors and are
receiver-only adaptations; this study does not reproduce the complete
published CDDM or ICDM training pipelines.

Section II defines the transmission model and receiver decision. Section III
presents the two candidates and selection rule, Section IV reports the
experiments, and Section V concludes the paper.

\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}
\subsection{Image Transmission with Co-Channel Interference}
We consider a desired image transmitter, a co-channel interferer, and a
receiver that reconstructs the desired image. Let $\bm u\in[0,1]^d$ denote a
vectorized RGB image, where $d=3H_{\rm img}W_{\rm img}$ for image height $H_{\rm img}$ and width $W_{\rm img}$. The frozen
source encoder $f_s$ maps $\bm u$ to $n$ complex channel symbols, and a separate
encoder $f_z$ maps an interfering image $\bm v$ to the same dimension:
\begin{equation}
 \bm x=\mathcal N(f_s(\bm u)),\qquad
 \bm z=\mathcal N(f_z(\bm v)),\quad
 \mathcal N(\bm t)=\frac{\sqrt n\,\bm t}{\norm{\bm t}}.
 \label{eq:encode}
\end{equation}
Thus $\norm{\bm x}^2=\norm{\bm z}^2=n$, and the channel bandwidth ratio is
$n/d$. The nonzero codewords are divided into $B$ contiguous blocks, with
$L_b$ symbols in block $b$ and $\sum_bL_b=n$.
The additive channel is
\begin{equation}
 \bm y_b=\bm x_b+a_b\bm z_b+\bm w_b,\qquad
 \bm w\sim\mathcal{CN}(\bm0,N_0\bm I),\quad a_b\ge0 ,
 \label{eq:channel}
\end{equation}
Here $a_b$ is the unknown interference amplitude in block $b$, and $N_0$ is
the complex AWGN power. We assume unit desired-channel gain and known symbol
alignment. The receiver knows $N_0$, the normalization convention, and its
block partition, but has no access to $a_b$, $\bm z$, or $\bm u$.
For a prescribed nominal SINR $\gamma$, the simulator sets average nominal
interference power to $10^{-\gamma/10}-N_0$. The instantaneous interference
energy also depends on the realized block energies of $\bm z$; nominal SINR
must therefore not be confused with measured per-frame SINR.

\subsection{Receiver Decision}
Let $\mathcal R_{\G}$ and $\mathcal R_{\SIC}$ denote Gaussian denoising and
joint signal--interference reconstruction, respectively. A selector
$\pi_\theta(\bm y)\in\{\G,\SIC\}$ chooses one receiver from the observation.
The common frozen decoder $g_s$ then reconstructs
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\bm u}_\theta
 =g_s\!\left(\mathcal N\!\left(\mathcal R_{\pi_\theta(\bm y)}(\bm y)\right)\right).
 \label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}
The design objective is to maximize mean per-image PSNR over a development
set by choosing $\theta$, with all encoder, decoder and diffusion weights
held fixed. Only the chosen branch is evaluated online. This separates the
receiver decision from codec retraining and makes its additional inference
cost explicit.

\section{Blind Disturbance-Model Selection}
\subsection{Candidate Disturbance Models}
The explicit model associates two learned priors with \eqref{eq:channel}:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 p(\bm x,\bm z\mid\bm y,\bm a)&\propto p_s(\bm x)p_z(\bm z)\\
 &\quad\times\exp\!\left[-\frac{1}{N_0}\sum_b
 \norm{\bm y_b-\bm x_b-a_b\bm z_b}^{2}\right].
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}
This equation describes the model; our finite-step sampler is an approximation,
not an exact posterior draw or a proven MAP optimizer.
Without priors, the change
$\bm x_b'=\bm x_b+a_b\bm\delta_b$,
$\bm z_b'=\bm z_b-\bm\delta_b$ leaves the mixture unchanged.
Power constraints remove some ambiguity but do not generally identify both
latents. The Gaussian model instead uses
\begin{equation}
 p_{\G}(\bm y\mid\bm x;\nu)=
 \mathcal{CN}(\bm y;\bm x,\nu\bm I),\qquad \nu\ge N_0 .
 \label{eq:gaussian}
\end{equation}
It sacrifices interference structure to avoid explicit component estimation.

\subsection{Candidate Receivers}
Both candidates process the same received frame and use the same desired-signal
prior and decoder. They differ in how they represent the disturbance.
For Gaussian reception, estimate the disturbance from raw received symbols:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 e_b=\frac{\norm{\bm y_b}^2}{L_b},\qquad
 \bar e=\frac{1}{n}\sum_b L_be_b,\\
 \widehat P=\pos{\bar e-1-N_0},\qquad
 \widehat\nu=N_0+\widehat P.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
With unit source power and vanishing signal--interference cross expectation,
unclipped excess energy estimates mean interference energy. Finite blocks,
latent correlations, and clipping prevent treating it as an exact amplitude
estimate. In particular, local desired-signal energy can resemble interference
heterogeneity.

Let $r(\bm y)\in\R^{2n}$ stack real and imaginary components. Given the training
diffusion schedule $\bar\alpha_t$, the Gaussian receiver $\G$ starts at the
nearest noise level satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1-\bar\alpha_{t_0}}{\bar\alpha_{t_0}}
 \simeq\frac{\widehat\nu}{2},\qquad
 \bm v_{t_0}=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t_0}}\,r(\bm y).
 \label{eq:match}
\end{equation}
The factor $1/2$ converts complex noise power to variance per real coordinate.
For decreasing respaced indices, a noise predictor $\epsilon_s$ gives
\begin{align}
 \widehat{\bm x}_0&=
 \frac{\bm v_t-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon_s(\bm v_t,t)}
 {\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}},\\
 \bm v_{t'}&=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t'}}\widehat{\bm x}_0+
 \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{t'}}\epsilon_s(\bm v_t,t).
 \label{eq:gaussian_update}
\end{align}
At most 40 predictor evaluations are used, with fewer when the matched initial
index is below 39. A common normalization and the frozen decoder produce the
reconstructed image.

The structured receiver $\SIC$ initializes
$a_b^{(0)}=\sqrt{\pos{e_b-1-N_0}}$ and performs joint diffusion inference with
signal and interference predictors. During late corrector updates it obtains
denoised, frame-normalized estimates $\widetilde{\bm x}$ and
$\widetilde{\bm z}$, then updates
\begin{equation}
 a_b^{+}=\pos{\frac{
 \Re\{\widetilde{\bm z}_b^{H}
       (\bm y_b-\widetilde{\bm x}_b)\}}
 {\norm{\widetilde{\bm z}_b}^{2}}}.
 \label{eq:amplitude}
\end{equation}
An empty denominator retains the previous amplitude. This is a nonnegative
least-squares update conditional on point estimates, not a correction for
their uncertainty. Calibration begins when the signal coefficient
$\alpha(t)\ge0.5$. Estimated block SINR updates the inherited guidance
coefficients by clipped linear interpolation of the original AWGN table.
We use the existing 40-step, order-three joint sampler: 40 signal and 40
interference evaluations. The initialization-only variant keeps amplitudes
fixed and supplies an ablation of calibration.

\subsection{Selection Before Generative Inference}
The selector augments $\widehat P$ with normalized block-energy variation
\begin{equation}
 H=\frac{B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^{B}(e_b-\bar e)^2}
          {\max(\bar e^2,\varepsilon)}.
 \label{eq:heterogeneity}
\end{equation}
Here $\varepsilon>0$ prevents division by zero, and all receiver blocks have
equal length. $H$ measures energy variation, not the interference distribution. For
$\theta=(\tau_\ell,\tau_h,\eta)$, define
\begin{equation}
 \pi_\theta(\bm y)=
 \begin{cases}
 \G,&\widehat P\le \tau_\ell,\ H\le\eta,\\
 \G,&\widehat P\le \tau_h,\ H>\eta,\\
 \SIC,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:selector}
\end{equation}
We also test the reversed assignment of $\G$ and $\SIC$ in
\eqref{eq:selector}; the Gaussian branch is not assumed to belong to the
low-power region. The energy-only family sets $\tau_\ell=\tau_h$. Either family can reduce to a
constant receiver; heterogeneity need not be useful.

We fit thresholds to mean per-image PSNR on development data. Eight folds keep
all channel conditions belonging to one image together. Each fold is predicted
using thresholds fitted on the other seven, then the family with the highest
out-of-fold mean is refitted on all development images. Ties prefer lower
component inference cost and then the simpler family. If neither family
improves on the better fixed receiver in development validation, that fixed
receiver is retained. No threshold is adjusted using confirmation or pressure
results. At reception, \eqref{eq:selector} is computed first and only its chosen
branch is executed. Selection adds $O(n)$ energy calculations and no diffusion
evaluations.

For analysis, let $q_j(\bm u,\bm y)$ be a candidate's PSNR. The unavailable
per-frame oracle has quality $\max(q_\G,q_\SIC)$. The exact identity
\begin{equation}
 \max(q_\G,q_\SIC)-q_{\pi_\theta}
 =|q_\G-q_\SIC|\,
 \mathbf1\{\pi_\theta\ne\arg\max_j q_j\}
 \label{eq:regret}
\end{equation}
shows that complementary receivers are necessary but not sufficient: mistakes
on large-gap frames matter most. This is an accounting identity, not a
generalization bound. Mean PSNR is the fitting objective; neither mean MSE,
LPIPS nor harmful-tail probability is implicitly optimized.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}
Desired images are $128\times128$ CelebA center crops \cite{liu2015celeba};
$32\times32$ CIFAR-10 interference images are nearest-neighbor upsampled.
Training uses 162,770 desired and 50,000 interference images. The frozen
SwinJSCC codec has stage widths $(128,192,256)$, depths $(2,2,6)$, and an
$8\times16\times16$ real latent: 1,024 complex symbols at CBR $1/48$.
Each DiT prior has 28 blocks, width 1,152, 16 heads and patch size two.
The inherited training schedule uses 40/100 desired/interference-codec epochs
and 50/20 desired/interference-prior epochs; selection adds no neural training.

DeepJSCC and MambaJSCC direct-codec references each train desired and
interference codecs for 40 epochs at SNR 20 dB and CBR $1/48$, using one seed
and validation MSE for checkpoint selection. DeepJSCC uses a pinned third-party
PyTorch reproduction; MambaJSCC uses official code. Public checkpoints are
also evaluated without fine-tuning, with one encoder processing both image
streams. Public MambaJSCC differs in training domain, channel and resolution;
public DeepJSCC additionally uses CBR $0.16256$. Their results are transfer
diagnostics. The SwinJSCC-specific diffusion priors cannot be attached to
these codecs without new prior training; complete published CDDM/ICDM systems
are not reproduced here.

Development uses 64 validation pairs and seed 1700. Independent confirmation
uses 256 test pairs, seeds 2700/2701, SNR 20 dB, and nominal SINRs
$\{-7,-4,0,4,7,10\}$ dB. Sixteen 64-symbol blocks have stationary,
alternating $(0.25,1.75)$, or burst $(0,2)$ relative interference powers.
The 18 conditions give 9,216 frames per receiver. Confirmation follows policy
freezing. File hashes exclude overlap with development, pressure tests and
200 previously evaluated pairs; independence is at image-pair, not identity,
level. The power grid is $\{0,0.1,0.2,0.35,0.5,0.75,1,1.5,2,3,4,8,10^6\}$;
the heterogeneity grid is $\{0.05,0.1,0.2,0.4,0.8,1.6\}$, giving 26 energy-only
and 2,028 two-feature rules across both assignment directions.

Controls include direct decoding, Gaussian reception using $N_0$ alone,
blind Gaussian reception, initialization-only and calibrated joint sampling,
and the selector. The same-gate control uses direct decoding for
$\widehat P\le0.15$ and calibrated joint sampling otherwise. Its outputs and
component times are reconstructed from paired branches, excluding gate
overhead. Development also tests coupled feedback and a Gaussian receiver
given nominal disturbance power, a privileged diagnostic. All SwinJSCC variants
share received tensors and sampler seeds; external codecs share image pairs,
conditions and channel seeds but generate their own codewords.

Measurements use float32, batch one, TF32 disabled, four CPU threads and an
RTX 4090. Synchronized receiver time includes selection, sampling and decoding;
encoding, metric computation and loading are excluded. The number of function
evaluations (NFE) counts calls to both priors.
Quality metrics are per-image PSNR and MSE, LPIPS-VGG v0.1 \cite{zhang2018lpips},
and four-scale MS-SSIM \cite{wang2003msssim}, averaged arithmetically after RGB
clipping to $[0,1]$; four scales fit the image resolution. Paired 95\% intervals
use 5,000 bootstrap resamples of image pairs, retaining both seeds and all
conditions. A harmful decision loses more than 0.5 dB against its comparator.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\input{generated/conclusion_result.tex}
The gain is supported for the evaluated source, interference population and
inference budget. It is not a guarantee for individual frames: the harmful
tail persists, and block-boundary mismatch nearly removes the advantage over
Gaussian denoising. Received-energy heterogeneity can also originate from the
desired signal, so the branch label should be interpreted as a reconstruction
decision rather than identification of the interferer.

PSNR and MSE aggregate the same pixel errors differently; LPIPS and MS-SSIM
add perceptual evidence but do not measure success in a driving, recognition
or control task. Applying this receiver decision to vehicular communication
requires evaluation with task-specific losses, fading, channel estimation and
synchronization. The present results establish a narrower point: selecting
between existing disturbance models can improve average reconstruction while
reducing the cost of always invoking joint inference.
